## The problem

Applicant information arrives through forms, email, calls, spreadsheets, and job boards. Staff copy it between systems, chase missing documents, send repetitive updates, and reconstruct reports. Adding another platform can make that worse when the real bottleneck has not been measured.

The diagnostic starts with the workflow, its exceptions, and its business cost—not a predetermined tool.

## What the $750 diagnostic includes

- One 45-minute workflow interview with the process owner.
- A current-state map covering systems, handoffs, exceptions, approvals, and data sensitivity.
- A conservative baseline: volume, operator time, wait time, misses/rework, and software/API cost.
- One recommended automation boundary with manual fallback, monitoring, security, and retention requirements.
- A fixed-scope implementation option or a clear recommendation not to automate.
- A 30-minute readout and a reusable written report.

Delivery target: five business days after the interview and required evidence are available.

## Good first workflows

- Applicant intake completeness and document routing.
- Status reminders that remain human-approved.
- Interview or shift scheduling handoffs.
- Candidate/client status reporting.
- Job intake normalization and internal routing.
- Missing-document and exception queues.

The service does not automate hiring decisions, make employment recommendations, scrape protected systems, or send unapproved messages.
